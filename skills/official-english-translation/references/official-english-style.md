# Official English Style

## Voice

Use polished, confident, externally publishable English. Sound clear, credible, and commercially useful. Avoid overdecorated language, inflated claims, and literal Chinese sentence structure.

Approved public style samples have priority. For brochures, PPTs, case studies, and subtitles, match the tone and structure in `official-translation-style-samples.md`: concise English headlines, commercial case-study framing, short subtitle lines, and natural scenario descriptions.

## Translation Approach

- Translate meaning, positioning, and audience impact before syntax.
- Prefer concise verbs and concrete nouns.
- Convert long Chinese lists into scannable English only when the output format allows it.
- Keep technical specificity where it matters; simplify only marketing filler.
- Preserve the source's factual scope. Do not add claims, customer promises, or guarantees.

## Marketing Copy

- Headlines: short, benefit-led, and natural.
- Subheads: explain value or scope, not just repeat the headline.
- CTAs: use standard English forms such as "Contact Us", "Learn More", "Request a Demo", or the user's preferred wording.
- Case-study results: use restrained, evidence-based language.
- Case studies: follow the historical flow: Real-World Applications -> Addressing Critical Challenges -> Core Capabilities -> Real-world Impact.
- Video subtitles: keep lines short enough to read on screen; use natural English action phrasing instead of literal Chinese syntax.

## Capitalization and Naming

- Use approved capitalization from `company-terminology.tsv`.
- Use Title Case for major slide titles and brochure section headings when the source design uses headline style.
- Use sentence case for body copy, captions, and long explanatory headings unless the existing document style differs.
- Do not translate product names, solution names, slogans, certification names, award names, or customer labels when an approved English name exists.
- Preserve exact spelling, spacing, capitalization, and trademark styling from the approved terminology table.

## Punctuation and Formatting

- Use English punctuation.
- Use US English spelling unless the user asks for another variant.
- Preserve units and numeric values. Convert units only when requested or clearly needed for the target market.
- Keep trademark symbols, registration marks, and legal disclaimers if present.

## Common Chinese-to-English Adjustments

- Replace "empower" only when it genuinely fits; avoid using it as a default for every "赋能".
- Translate "打造" by context: "build", "create", "develop", "establish", or rewrite around the outcome.
- Translate "落地" by context: "implement", "deploy", "put into practice", or "deliver measurable results".
- Translate "解决方案" as the approved solution name when listed in the glossary; otherwise use "solution" naturally.
- Translate "案例" as "case study" for external promotional material.
- For logistics tasks, prefer "handling" or "material handling" over generic "moving" when the context is operational.
- For damaged structures in challenge-course subtitles, prefer natural operational phrasing over literal word-for-word translation.
- For remote inspection subtitles, use concise action phrasing and idiomatic time/location expressions.
- For robot challenge-course headings, use compact Title Case such as "Robot Obstacle Course Highlights".
