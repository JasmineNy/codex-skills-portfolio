# Translation QA Checklist

Use this checklist before delivering official English promotional materials.

## Terminology

- Every source term in `company-terminology.tsv` uses its approved English equivalent.
- No forbidden or legacy translation appears in the final English text.
- New candidate terms are listed for approval instead of silently becoming official terms.
- Product, solution, service, certification, award, and slogan capitalization is consistent.

## Accuracy

- Company names, customer names, partner names, dates, numbers, certifications, awards, and metrics match the source.
- Claims do not exceed what the source says.
- Ambiguous or missing source information is flagged.
- Legal or compliance-sensitive text is translated conservatively.

## Publication Quality

- The English reads naturally for an international audience.
- Headlines, subheads, captions, and CTA lines fit their medium.
- The translation avoids literal idioms and machine-translation phrasing.
- Layout-sensitive text is short enough for slides, brochures, and charts.
- Brochures, PPTs, case studies, and subtitles match the historical style samples in `official-translation-style-samples.md` unless the user requested a different style.
- Case-study copy follows the established Real-World Applications / Addressing Critical Challenges / Core Capabilities / Real-world Impact pattern when the source is a formal case study.
- Subtitle copy is concise, natural, and readable on screen.

## Artifact Integrity

- PPT slide order, speaker notes, chart labels, image captions, and footers are preserved.
- Brochure section hierarchy, callouts, tables, and disclaimers are preserved.
- Case studies retain Client / Challenge / Solution / Results logic.
- Final handoff includes unresolved terminology or assumptions.
